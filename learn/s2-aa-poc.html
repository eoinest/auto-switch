Keep the S2 beside the breadboard.

Use female-to-male jumper leads. Its two adjacent header rows can short together if inserted into the same breadboard strips. Drawing uses the LOLIN S2 Mini reference; compare your actual board’s labels.

Unplug the S2 jumpers before USB.

Remove all three S2 leads before USB programming. Its 5V/VBUS pin is tied directly to USB power: the holder’s OFF switch alone does not isolate the servo rail.

Measure first.

With the S2 and servo disconnected, measure about 5.0 V between the breadboard rails. Switch off before connecting them. First test with the servo horn unloaded.

BUILD CHECKLIST

Twelve wires, including two rail bridges.

P/G numbers count rail holes from the top. The main strips use a–j and 1–63. Adapt to your breadboard only after checking its unpowered continuity.

THE SELECTED PARTS

Use what you already have.

  • ESP32-S2 Mini: your board, using WEMOS reference pinout.
  • DAIERTEK switched 4-AA holder: selected Amazon case, with four Amazon Basics 1.5 V alkaline cells.
  • Teyleten 5 V buck-boost: selected module. Product photo says XL63070; listing says TPS63070. Verify the delivered labels and output.
  • One MG90S, breadboard, jumpers, solder and insulation: reuse your supplies. Three female-to-male S2 leads; five male-to-male leads for servo and bridges; two soldered converter output leads.

Firmware in the existing Pico profile has not yet been ported or tested on this S2 board. This page is a wiring reference and never controls hardware.

BLENDER / FIRST MECHANICAL PROTOTYPE

A wall actuator and a separate electronics carrier.

The battery gets its own support, reducing weight on the switch mount. The bench breadboard stays outside this carrier. The purple board uses the official S2 outline; the pink bay reserves space for the converter until its dimensions are measured.
