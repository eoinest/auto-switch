BUILD IT ON THE BENCH

A hole-by-hole breadboard layout.

Open full-size layout ↗

Use your Pico with soldered headers and a standard 830-point, 63-row breadboard. USB points up; headers occupy c3–c22 and h3–h22. Power rails stay unused. The motor power harness sits beside the board; its cards show named connections, not the physical locations of module pads.

100%Zoom to read the row numbers. J labels match the table below.

Each jumper plugs into exactly its two named holes. Its drawn route may pass over other holes without connecting. Component lead bends and cable lengths are illustrative; the hole assignments are exact. Fit and powered operation still need bench checks.

What to add for the bench

One 830-point breadboard and one 20-pack of 150 mm male/male jumpers. Reuse your headered Pico and the circuit components already in the BOM. Six jumper wires can become soldered leads to the external harness; keep the thicker motor wires already specified.

Why these holes work, and fact-check sources

A breadboard joins a–e across each numbered row and f–j across the same row separately. We deliberately avoid the side power rails and their possible midpoint breaks. The Pico’s two headers are 17.78 mm apart, matching c/h placement across the trench. An independent checker joins those copper strips and our jumpers, then compares the result with the project wiring nets.
